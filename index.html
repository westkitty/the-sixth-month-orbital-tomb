WASD moves; mouse looks around after clicking the world. Shift moves faster. In free float use Q/E to rise/sink, Z/C to roll, and B to toggle braking. Mouse wheel adjusts orbital distance.

G shows original structural geometry, T traces your path, V compares temporal states, F anchors, R recovers, K traces the reconstructed distance from a selected structure's original position to its current position. Y toggles in-world amber/cyan/grey provenance markers. In the instruments, test three evidence relationships after inspecting the required physical locations, ride a room-to-orbit scale transition, or opt into paced witness holds. The entry screen also offers an instrument-free beginning; H restores controls. Adaptive rendering quality can be disabled. All these tools are reconstructions, not recovered crew equipment. Comma and period witness the previous or next modeled separation window at 0.5x and pause automatically near its joint. [ and ] step months. Space pauses or plays, - / = changes speed, J watches active separations, L boosts visibility, I inspects the nearest physical trace or missing coordinate. H hides the interface; Escape releases pointer lock.

Inspection markers and in-room structural displays are model-derived survey reconstructions, not preserved crew messages. Their information changes with the same geometry you can walk through. The absence of a room can be inspected from its former position, even when the room itself no longer exists.

The seven months are experience checkpoints, not an archival assertion about exact dates. The original room location is held in world coordinates after its structure departs. Earlier routes represent only your own movements.

Collision stops movement through intact hulls and machinery, including in free float. When a floor detaches, free float safely replaces walking. WATCH SEPARATIONS switches to an orbital tracking view; electrical cut arcs occur only at the separating joints.

Weather simulations are speculative and restricted to a bounded atmospheric observation volume; they do not occur in station vacuum. Sound is optional and silent in open vacuum.
