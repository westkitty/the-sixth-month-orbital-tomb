# R9 — Touch Orbit and Input Recovery

**THE SIXTH MONTH / ORBITAL TOMB** is a standalone, source-conscious Three.js 0.185.1 exploration experience. This version extends R8; it is not a museum exhibit and does not amend Starsilk canon.

R9 adds **TWO-FINGER ORBITAL INSPECTION**: single touch rotates the world camera; a second touch becomes pinch zoom, capped at the same camera-distance bounds as the mouse wheel. Lifting either finger recalibrates without a camera snap, and pointer loss/cancellation clears the correct touch ID. Mouse pointer lock now tolerates browsers that return void instead of a Promise; simulated touch clicks do not request pointer lock. The original keyboard, gamepad-style touch buttons, navigation modes and one render loop remain. This is input quality, not a new lore event.

R8 added **FOCUSED TEMPORAL EXPOSURE**: select a real station component and activate ISOLATE SELECTED COMPARISON. Only the source-derived earlier-time mesh for that component remains visible; change the existing comparison month to examine before/after structure without obscuring all of Meridian. Disable the focus to restore the full-station comparison. Stored selection is validated against actual stable IDs, and the new preference safely normalizes old saves. This is a spatial comparison of the existing reconstruction, never historical footage.

R7 added **PHYSICAL FRAGMENT ANCHORING**: F/ANCHOR now records a local position on a real station component. As that component drifts or rotates in the reversible model, the player rides it; release preserves the actual world coordinate and restores free float as appropriate. Anchors never attach to ghost/comparison meshes, and rider motion does not falsely become a recorded walking path. This is not a claim that historical crew rode the debris.

R6 added **SEAM WITNESSING**: press period or NEXT CUT to enter the next reconstructed disassembly window, or comma/PREV CUT for the previous one. The camera begins on a real original attachment joint, plays at 0.5x, and halts while that group is visibly separating. The six windows come from the existing removal-model buckets, cover all 74 removable model groups exactly once, and never invent historical dates or extra people. These are model groups, **not** a canon casualty count. Standard camera, seeking, playback and accessibility controls override witness mode without losing saved work. This removes blind seeking and makes the structural sequence legible as an event instead of another timeline label. Electrical cut arcs now freeze with the same reversible time coordinate, instead of continuing to flicker indefinitely through a paused cut.

R5 added a **reconstructed displacement survey**: select a real removable structure, press K (or TRACE DISPLACEMENT in the tracking instruments), and observe a sparse in-world vector between its original coordinates and its current modeled position. The vector contracts on reverse scrub, never appears for unmoved or permanently surviving pieces, and vanishes when the cinematic HUD is hidden. Its endpoints derive from the exact same geometry transforms as the live station. It is **not** a historical flight recorder or a supernatural connection. The separation camera now stays on one active joint until that joint stops separating, instead of jumping between re-sorted active events. At the final checkpoint it frames the original observation coordinates. Visibility settings also persist across Virgil atmospheric updates and restore correctly after probe storms.

R4 originally added **physical inspection nodes** tied to station pieces, **revisitable absence coordinates**, **six named remains as physical but non-speaking landmarks**, and **in-room structural instruments** whose readings change with the same timeline that moves actual geometry. Low-frequency structural stress is only audible by contact with surviving compartments. ## The core experience

Begin walking in Meridian's observation room. Witness each source-derived removal with NEXT CUT or the period key, and use PREV CUT or comma to revisit an earlier seam. Walk the radial corridor and remember its path. Press **K** after selecting a station component to reveal the modeled distance from its original position to its current position. Press **I** near a physical inspection point to see source-classified structural evidence. The interface offers the same action as a touch-friendly **INSPECT** button when near evidence. Press **3** to survey from orbit, select an identified structure and inspect it; press **J** to watch active separation joints. Time moves by controls and `Space`, with `-` / `=` changing speed. Witness docking arms leave before communications and habitation. Each witness window stops automatically at an active separation so you can examine it. Return with **4** to the original observation coordinates after Month Six. The instrument can inspect **absence** there even though the observation room itself has left. Press **G** for the original geometry, **T** for your earlier walking trace, toggle **G** and **T** off again if desired, then press **H** to hide interface instrumentation. The final scene must stand alone.

**The inspection readouts describe model-derived evidence. They are not recovered crew dialogue, transmissions from the dead, or newly established canon.** Named remains stay silent and inert.

- All functions remain available in keyboard-accessible on-screen controls; the selected atmospheric profile exists only inside Virgil's observation probe, never in vacuum. Reduce-motion and contrast preferences are supported.
